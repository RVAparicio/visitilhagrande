If you're planning to reach **Ilha Grande from Rio de Janeiro**, this guide is for you. It's not another "how to do it on the cheap" piece — the kind that, deep down, means losing two days to save a few reais. It's the opposite: how to do it right. Written by someone who has lived on the island for almost seven years.

## Before you come: this isn't a beach resort you fly into

One thing worth understanding before you book anything: **Ilha Grande is unlike any other beach spot in Brazil.** It isn't Rio, and it isn't one of those resort towns where you step off the plane and you're already on the sand. It's an **isolated island** — no cars, no bridges, no airport. Everything moves **on foot or by boat**.

What does that mean in practice? Two things. First, you **will** make at least one transfer to get here — there's no way around it. Second, some day the island **will "cost you" one**: you might get a day of bad weather stuck indoors, or a boat tour that can't sail because the sea won't allow it. In Rio you go for seven days and you're in Rio for seven days, full stop; here the rhythm is different — and that's exactly the charm. So the first thing to sort out isn't the flight or the hotel, it's **how many nights you give it** (spoiler: more than you're thinking).

## Getting from Rio to Ilha Grande

Rio de Janeiro is your gateway — you fly into **Galeão (GIG)**, Rio's international airport, and cross to the island from there. Here's the detail that changes the trip, and it's **different going out and coming back**:

| Leg (going out) | Best option | Rough price |
|---|---|---|
| **As a couple** | Shared transfer, door to pier | ~R$200 per person |
| **Three or more** | Uber to the Conceição de Jacareí pier + speedboat | ~R$230 for the car, plus boat |

> Prices are approximate and change with the season — treat them as a guide, not a quote.

**On the way out**, if you're a couple, a shared transfer door-to-pier and you can forget about the logistics. If you're three or more, Rio has Ubers to spare: it usually pays to ride down to the pier at Conceição de Jacareí and take the speedboat from there. One tip: set the exact pier and route in the app, so no driver can overcharge you "because the destination was somewhere else."

**On the way back (island → Rio), take the transfer — always**, because here Uber turns on you. Conceição and Angra have nothing like Rio's supply of cars, and there's a hustle: drivers mark themselves "available" half an hour before the boat lands, then vanish the moment they see it come in — and whoever's left charges a fortune. The transfer, by contrast, leaves at a fixed time and three hours later you're at your hotel door in Rio, in one piece.

## Money: the thing nobody warns you about

**In Brazil, Pix rules.** It's the instant bank-transfer system, and locals pay for everything with it — right down to a coconut on the beach. As a visitor, two moves save you money and headaches:

1. **If you pay by card, get charged in reais — not your home currency.** When a machine offers to bill you in dollars, euros or pounds ("dynamic currency conversion"), it's quietly adding a worse exchange rate on top of whatever your bank already charges. Always choose reais.
2. **Bring some cash in reais before you cross.** There is **no ATM and no exchange office** on the island. In Vila do Abraão most restaurants and pousadas take cards, but beach kiosks and the remoter corners are often cash-only — and there, Pix or cash is all that works.

## How many nights to stay (and where)

**Stay at least five nights.** And if you can, **seven or more** is ideal. With five, the odds work in your favour: one may be rainy and the rest are usually spectacular. With two or three, if you hit bad weather, you leave without ever seeing the island.

**And sleep in Vila do Abraão.** It's the hub: everything leaves on foot from here — restaurants, caiçara and international food, live music, four supermarkets. There are beautiful corners to spend a day (Araçatiba, Bananal, Praia Vermelha), but to *sleep* they leave you isolated, with one boat a day and everything pricier. ## Common mistakes (so you don't make them)

- **Assuming everything in Brazil is close.** This is the one I see most. You land in Rio, come down here — about 130 km, a couple of hours — and then think "right, now I'll pop over to Búzios"… which is 400-plus kilometres away, in the exact opposite direction. The Rio region runs on two axes: **Rio + north** (Búzios, Arraial do Cabo, Cabo Frio) or **Rio + south** (Ilha Grande, Paraty). It isn't "Rio or the island, all or nothing" — it's about choosing an axis. Two destinations in two weeks is perfect (say, five days in Rio and seven nights here); but three places in opposite directions means spending your holiday in a car.
- **Coming for fewer than five nights.** We said it already, but it's one of the worst: miss the weather on one day and you're left without the island.
- **Skimping on the transfer.** Saving five reais on a bus only to lose fifteen on a taxi — and a whole afternoon — is the worst economy there is. A holiday is **your quality time**: it's when you step out of the routine that grips you all year, when you rest, eat well, treat yourself, clear your head. Don't trade that for loose change.
